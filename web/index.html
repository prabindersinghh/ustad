On the factory floor

Your most valuable procedures retire with your senior staff.

Changeovers, tank cleaning, tinting-machine calibration, lockout before maintenance: the jobs that decide whether a batch ships live in the heads of a few senior technicians. A new operator learns them by shadowing, by trial, and by scrap.

The laminated SOP on the wall says what should happen. Nobody checks whether it did until QC rejects the batch or the line goes down. Ustad puts your senior technician's know-how on every shift, and a second pair of eyes on every step.

How it works

Record the job once. Run it right on every shift.

Capture, for your senior technician

Do the job once, talking it through

Wear the glasses or a chest-mounted phone and work as normal. Step cards build on screen as you talk. Ustad turns what you do and say into a job card: the order, the exact settings and torque values you mention, the safety warning before each risky step, and what a finished step should look like to a camera. Before saving, it checks every step against your own footage and rewrites any step that names a part it can't find.

Guide, for the operator on the line

Hands busy, eyes on the work

The operator hears one step at a time and sees the part to work on outlined live in their view. They say "done" and Ustad looks before it agrees. Right: it moves on and saves a photo. Wrong: it says what is wrong and how to fix it, and holds the job there. When a step is plainly finished, it notices without being asked.

The product

The real screens, on a real machine

No mockups. These are the app's own screens from a job on a pad-printing machine, with the factory clip fed in as the camera.

Every job leaves a record

Each check saves its photo, verdict and reason in a printable evidence pack. A supervisor can also ask a second model to re-check a finished job, or only its doubtful steps.

The loop

What happens when an operator says "done"

Reading a checklist aloud is easy. Ustad looks at the work itself, decides whether the step is really done, and acts on that decision before the operator moves on.

  1. 1
    See

    The operator's view, already streaming, is the frame that gets judged.

  2. 2
    Ground

    SAM 3.1 finds the step's target part in that frame and answers with coordinates and a confidence, not an opinion.

  3. 3
    Judge

    Muse Spark weighs the frame, the grounding and the success criteria, and must commit to exactly one action.

  4. 4
    Act

    Move on and save the photo, correct the operator, or ask for a clearer view. Guessing is not an option.

Why it's different

Built for the floor, not for a demo

It never passes a step it didn't see

If the camera can't see the valve clearly, Ustad asks the operator to move instead of guessing. On a running line, a confident wrong pass costs far more than a two-second pause.

Gloves on, hands full

Operators hold tools and wear gloves, so nothing needs a tap. They just talk: "done", "repeat", "what's wrong", "where is it". Ustad answers out loud and outlines the part on screen.

The shift log writes itself

Every checked step saves a timestamped photo and the reason it passed or failed. A finished job is the record your QC lead and auditor ask for, with no checklist filled in from memory at the end of the shift.

Ask Ustad, out loud

"Which one?" "It won't turn." Ustad answers from the expert's own words and the live view, and lights up the part. "Can I skip the lockout?" goes to a supervisor.

English and Hindi

A worker can run the whole job in Hindi. Only the words change: the check stays the same, and numbers in every translation are verified in code.

Safety checks in code

Lockout and hazard steps are never credited from a later view. A hand too close to a hazard gets a spoken warning, while the job carries on.

Works through dropouts

If the Wi-Fi drops mid-recording, the phone keeps going and syncs when it reconnects. Mid-job, the worker carries on and resumes at the exact step.

Job cards that improve

Ustad reads where workers failed, asked and skipped, and proposes edits. A supervisor approves each one, and each approval makes a new version.

A view for supervisors

A dashboard shows every job, every check and what it cost, with the photo behind each verdict and a printable pack for your auditors.

A checklist tells you what should have happened. Ustad tells you what did.

Every step is checked against what the camera actually saw, not what someone ticked at the end of the shift.

Questions

Straight answers

Does it work on Meta glasses today?

Not yet. Ustad is built for Meta glasses, and today it runs in the browser on a phone clipped to the chest, which gives the same camera, microphone and speaker. It hasn't been tested on glasses. The concept view above shows where it's going.

What do we need to install?

Nothing on your machines and nothing wired into your PLCs. It runs in a phone's browser.

How long does setup take?

An expert records the job once. In our test runs, a short recording became a checked job card in 17 to 34 seconds. There's no training data to collect for each machine.

Does video of our line leave the plant?

Today, frames go to the hosted Meta Model API. Running the checks on a GPU inside the plant is the plan for sites that need it, and that part isn't tested yet.

What if the camera can't see the part?

Ustad asks for a better view instead of guessing. If its vision model can't find the part, the record shows that no box was found.

How much does it cost to run?

In our own test runs, about $0.08 to record a job and about $0.10 to guide a 4-step job, in model costs.

Which languages does it speak?

English and Hindi today. Real accents and factory noise in Hindi still need more testing.

Has it been used in a real factory?

We tested it in two: a textile clothing factory, and MCPL (Modern Colours Private Limited) in Ludhiana. Trial figures are in progress, and the numbers on this page come from our own test runs.
